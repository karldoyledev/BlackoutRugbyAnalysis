This is not a redesign. It is the evidence for one: every visible /Squad card appears in its Today form (as built) next to alternative treatments. Judge each card individually — switch variants with the chips, stack them all with the bottom-right toggle.

For each card, answer three things: what did you originally want it to answer? · does it earn its place? · which option is closest — or none? Your answers land on the card's ticket (linked in each judge block); the page's primary job is derived from them, not assumed.

C1 · Control panel

How data arrives, and what the top of the page is. Infrastructure — redesigned only as a consequence of this decision.

Today — manual load form (pre-Club-Link legacy)

Nothing renders until you press the button. The endpoint/Team fields are dead weight — your credentials ride the Club Link; Season 50–80 is a hardcoded list. Nothing here says whose page this is.

B — Club strip · page renders from cache on visit, capture is explicit

Ashgrove RFC Team CSR 71 Season 80

Arriving on the page renders everything cached immediately (no button required). Capture squad is the only live read: roster refresh (2 calls) + a Squad Snapshot save — the volatile present. The season select scopes aggregates. Cold start (no snapshot yet) shows a single "Capture squad" bootstrap panel, mirroring /Players.

Judge C1 — Is the control panel a tool (manual load) or a context strip (club + capture)? Which makes you want to visit the page? → resolution lands on S2.

C3 · Squad changes

Who changed, since when. Five snapshots exist in sample data (Aug 2 → Sep 21); today's card reads exactly the newest two.

Today — delta vs. your previous load (whenever that was)

Two loads five minutes apart and two loads three weeks apart render identically. The amber hidden in today's build rows are players whose ONLY movement was salary or form — the current filter silently drops them. Performance deltas here compare season aggregates at capture time, so "played fixtures since the last load" reads as "the player changed".

B — chosen baseline · salary & form included · joined/left grouped · CSR history per player

Baseline is a choice, not an accident. Each row keeps a five-capture CSR sparkline so "improving" and "declining" are visible at a glance; joined/left get their own sections; every delta that moved is shown. Player names link to Player History.

Judge C3 — What did you originally want: who joined/left, who improved or declined, or contract movement — all three? Is "since previous load" ever the right baseline, or must it be a choice? → resolution lands on S3.

C4 · Player comparison

The squad ranked. Today's selector mixes present-state attributes (CSR, energy, form, salary, age — the live read) with season aggregates in one ~40-stat list. (Sample data exposes 10 of them.)

Today — one dropdown, 23 ranked bars, no context

Switching from "Tackles" to "CSR" silently changes what the bars MEAN (a season's work vs. right now). A bar that says "14" never says whether 14 is good for this squad.

B — curated tabs aligned to the app's 7 Stat Groups · median marker · top-5 emphasized

Present-state (General) and season aggregates (the four Stat-Group tabs) no longer hide in one list — the tab tells you what kind of question you're asking. The amber line is the squad median: instantly visible is who is carrying and who is drifting.

C5 · Fixture breakdown

One Fixture at a time. Today: 7 silently-chosen metrics, team and players sharing one selector. Sample per-player fixture stats are generated (deterministic) for the 23 squad rows.

Today — team totals and one player's output render in the same shape

"Entire team" (78 tackles) and "G. Trent" (6 tackles) share an axis and a chart type. The 7 metrics were never justified — they cut across the Stat Groups used everywhere else in the app.

B — subject split: team mode shows top contributors per metric; player mode benchmarks vs. team average

Team mode answers "how did the team break down, and who drove it". Player mode answers "did X pull their weight vs. the team's average output".

C — fixture sheet: the whole XV by Stat-Group columns, sortable

A table, not a chart — every player visible at once, columns grouped by Stat Group. Click a header to sort.

Judge C5 — What did you originally want: "who did what in match N", "how did the team break down", or a selection aid for the next Fixture? Does it survive Player History owning per-Player trends and Match Analysis owning diagnosis? → resolution lands on S5.
